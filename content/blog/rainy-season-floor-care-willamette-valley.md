Good commercial floor care in the rainy season comes down to three things: stop water and grit at the door with enough matting, keep walking surfaces dry during business hours, and clean each floor type the way it needs to be cleaned, more often than you would in summer. In the Willamette Valley that plan needs to run for most of the year, not just a few stormy weeks.

## Why valley floors take a beating from October to May

Rain in the valley is not a few storms. It is a season. The National Weather Service's [Climate of Eugene summary](https://www.weather.gov/media/pqr/climate/ClimateBookEugene/pg1.pdf) notes that nearly 90 percent of the area's annual rainfall falls between early October and mid-May, and only about 2 percent in July and August.

For floors, that means months of:

- **Water tracked in** on shoes, umbrellas, strollers and hand trucks
- **Mud and fine grit** from parking strips, landscaping and job sites
- **Gravel** carried in from lots and rural driveways, which scratches finished floors like sandpaper
- **Wet entries** that stay slick long after the person who tracked the water in has gone

A Corvallis storefront on a downtown street, an Albany office in an older building with original hardwood, a Salem agency lobby and a busy Eugene clinic all deal with the same problem. The difference is how much traffic they get and what their floors are made of.

## Wet floors are a safety issue, not just a cleaning issue

A wet entry is a slip waiting to happen. For employers, keeping floors clean and dry is also a workplace safety requirement. Federal OSHA's walking-working surfaces rule, [29 CFR 1910.22](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.22), requires workroom floors to be kept clean and, to the extent feasible, dry, and walking-working surfaces to be kept free of hazards such as leaks and spills. Oregon OSHA adopts that general requirement by reference in its [Division 2, Subdivision D rules](https://osha.oregon.gov/rules/final/division-2/Pages/div2-subdivision-D.aspx).

This is a summary, not legal advice. If you manage a workplace, check the current rules or talk to a safety professional about what applies to your building.

In practice, the cleaning side of compliance looks like this:

- Keep entry mats in place, flat and not saturated
- Put out wet-floor signs whenever a floor is damp
- Dry-mop or wipe up tracked water during the day, not just at closing
- Schedule wet mopping and machine work for after hours so floors are dry when people arrive

## Your rainy season commercial floor care checklist

Use this as a starting point and adjust it for your traffic.

### Entryways and matting

1. **Use a two-part mat system.** A coarse scraper mat outside for grit and gravel, and an absorbent wiper mat inside to catch water.
2. **Make the inside mat long enough** that people take several steps on it before they reach the bare floor. A small square in front of the door is not enough in January.
3. **Swap or dry saturated mats.** A soaked mat stops absorbing and starts spreading water. Rental mat services can rotate them.
4. **Vacuum mats regularly.** Grit trapped in a mat gets kicked back onto the floor if it is never removed.
5. **Keep an umbrella stand or bag station** at the main entrance.

### Daily and nightly floor work

- Dust-mop hard floors before wet-mopping, so grit is picked up instead of dragged
- Wet-mop high-traffic lanes nightly during the wettest months
- Vacuum carpeted entries and corridors more often than in summer
- Spot-clean muddy tracks as they happen during the day
- Wipe down baseboards near entries, where mop water and road film collect

### Periodic work

- Buff finished floors on a cycle matched to traffic so they keep their protective sheen
- Inspect for scratches, dull lanes and worn finish near the doors, and plan refinishing with a flooring contractor when needed
- Check that mats are not curling at the edges, which is its own trip hazard

## Hardwood, LVP and tile: what each needs in the wet months

Different floors fail in different ways. Always follow the flooring manufacturer's care instructions first.

### Hardwood

Water and grit are both enemies. Standing water can work into seams and cause swelling or cupping, and gravel scratches the finish. Use a barely damp mop, never a soaking one, and a cleaner made for finished wood. Dry-mop daily in high-traffic spots. Original hardwood in older downtown buildings, like those in Albany's historic districts, needs surface-appropriate products rather than a one-size mop.

### Luxury vinyl plank (LVP)

LVP handles water better than wood, but it is not invincible. Water can seep through seams at the edges, and fine grit dulls the wear layer. Use a pH-neutral cleaner and a damp mop, and avoid steam mops unless the manufacturer allows them.

### Tile and grout

Tile is durable, but wet tile is slippery, and grout lines hold mud. Mop with a neutral cleaner, rinse well so no residue is left to make it slicker, and scrub grout lines near entrances more often during the rainy season.

### Carpet

Carpeted entries hide a lot of water and grit. Vacuum high-traffic carpet frequently with a strong vacuum, and treat spots before they set. Deep extraction is a separate specialty service.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I keep my business entrance from getting slippery when it rains?

Keep the entrance dry with a two-part mat system, wet-floor signs and regular wipe-ups during business hours. Use a coarse scraper mat outside and a long absorbent mat inside, and replace mats once they are saturated. Schedule wet mopping after hours so floors are dry when people arrive. Federal and Oregon OSHA rules expect workroom floors to be kept clean and, where feasible, dry.

### How often should commercial floors be mopped in the rainy season?

Most high-traffic commercial floors in the Willamette Valley need wet-mopping nightly during the wettest months, with dust-mopping or spot cleaning during the day. Low-traffic offices may be fine with a few times a week. The right frequency depends on how many people come through, what the floors are made of and how well your entry matting is working.

### Can I use a wet mop on hardwood floors?

No, hardwood should only be cleaned with a barely damp mop, never a wet one. Standing water can seep into the seams and cause swelling or cupping over time. Dry-mop first to remove grit, use a cleaner made for finished wood, and always check the flooring manufacturer's care instructions before using any new product.

### How big should an entrance mat be for a business?

An entrance mat should be long enough that most people take several steps on it before reaching the bare floor. A single small mat at the door catches only a fraction of the water and grit. Pair a scraper mat outside with an absorbent mat inside, and make sure edges lie flat so the mat does not become a trip hazard.

### Is LVP flooring waterproof?

LVP is water resistant, but it is not fully waterproof as an installed floor. The planks themselves handle moisture well, but water can seep through seams and edges into the subfloor if it sits. Wipe up standing water promptly, use a damp rather than wet mop, and follow the manufacturer's guidance on cleaners and steam mops.
