Good rental turnover cleaning in Eugene comes down to three things: a written checklist every unit is held to, a realistic gap between one tenant's move-out and the next move-in, and a plan for summer, when leases near the University of Oregon end in a cluster. Get those right and units go back on the market faster, with fewer callbacks from new tenants. This guide walks through each one for landlords and property managers in Eugene and Springfield.

## Start with the move-out inspection, then clean

Before anyone cleans, walk the unit with the move-in condition report and photograph everything. That inspection is the record you will rely on if you claim part of the deposit. Under [ORS 90.300](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors090.html), Oregon landlords can deduct only what is reasonably necessary to remedy the tenant's defaults and repair damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, and must return the deposit or send a written accounting that states the specific basis of each claim within 31 days after the tenancy ends and possession is returned.

That 31-day clock runs alongside your turnover. If the cleaning crew finds something your inspection missed, you want to know quickly. A simple rule: inspect and photograph first, clean second, then photograph the clean unit for the next tenant's move-in file. This is a summary, not legal advice, so check the current statute or talk to a professional about specific deductions.

## The rental turnover cleaning standard every Eugene unit should meet

Write the standard down as a checklist and use it for every unit so it does not drift from crew to crew or season to season. A complete turnover covers:

### Kitchen

- Oven interior, racks, door glass and stovetop
- Range hood and filter
- Refrigerator and freezer inside and out, including seals and drawers
- Dishwasher door edges and filter area
- Inside and outside of every cabinet and drawer
- Sink, faucet, backsplash and countertops
- Floor, including under and behind movable appliances where accessible

### Bathrooms

- Tub, shower, grout, fixtures and door tracks
- Toilet inside and out, base and behind
- Vanity, mirror and medicine cabinet inside and out
- Exhaust fan cover dusted
- Floor and baseboards

### Living areas and bedrooms

- Ceiling fans, light fixtures and blinds dusted
- Interior window glass and window tracks
- Baseboards, door frames, doors and switch plates
- Closet shelves and floors
- Carpets vacuumed and spot-treated, hard floors mopped

### Before you hand over keys

1. Walk the unit against the checklist and photograph each room.
2. Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and note anything that needs a repair.
3. Confirm appliances work and are empty.
4. Check entries, patios and storage areas for anything left behind.

Keep repair items separate from the cleaning list. A cleaning crew should flag a dripping faucet or a broken blind, not be expected to fix it.

## Eugene's Rental Housing Code and what it means for turnovers

Eugene has its own Rental Housing Code on top of state law. Rental units in the city must be registered with the City's Rental Housing Program, and the code sets minimum habitability standards covering areas such as weatherproofing, plumbing, heating, smoke detection, security, electrical, structural integrity, carbon monoxide and appliances. The City's [landlord resources page](https://www.eugene-or.gov/5258/Landlord-Resources) has the registration details and current handbook.

Turnover is the natural time to check those systems, since the unit is empty and accessible. Cleaning does not make a unit compliant, but a crew working through every room is in a good position to notice problems worth a closer look: a missing smoke alarm cover, a sticking window, signs of a leak under a sink. Springfield properties fall under Springfield's own rules, so check with the City of Springfield for anything specific to units there.

## How much time to allow between tenants

There is no single right gap, but squeezing move-out and move-in into the same day rarely works for a full turnover. Plan for:

- **Inspection and photos** on the day keys come back
- **Repairs and paint** first, since they create dust and debris
- **The full clean** after the trades are done
- **A final walk-through** before the new tenant's keys are released

If repairs are light, the clean itself can usually be done in a day for a typical apartment. Units that need paint, flooring or appliance work need more lead time, and the clean should always come last so new dust is not left behind.

The University of Oregon's spring term ends in June, and a lot of rentals around campus and the West University neighborhood turn over around the same time. Rental and student-housing turnover in Eugene lands in concentrated bursts, and we plan crew capacity around those dates rather than discovering them.

1. List every unit's lease end date by early spring.
2. Book cleaning dates as soon as you know which tenants are leaving.
3. Stagger repairs so trades and cleaners are not in the same unit at once.
4. Leave a buffer day before each new move-in for anything that slips.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does rental turnover cleaning in Eugene take?

Rental turnover cleaning in Eugene usually takes about a day for a typical apartment once the unit is empty and repairs are finished. Larger homes, heavily used kitchens or units with extra build-up take longer. The bigger time factor is sequencing: inspection, then repairs and paint, then the clean, then a final walk-through before the next tenant arrives.

### Should cleaning happen before or after repairs?

Cleaning should happen after repairs and painting. Patching, sanding, painting and flooring work all create dust and debris that settle on surfaces a crew has already cleaned. Schedule trades first, then the full clean, then a final walk-through. Ask the crew to flag anything they notice that still needs a fix.

### Do Eugene rental units need to be registered with the city?

Yes. Rental units in Eugene must be registered with the City's Rental Housing Program, which also enforces minimum habitability standards under the Rental Housing Code. Check the City of Eugene's landlord resources for current registration requirements. Springfield properties follow Springfield's own rules, so confirm separately for units there.

### Can a landlord charge a tenant for turnover cleaning in Oregon?

A landlord can deduct cleaning costs from a deposit only where the unit was left in worse condition than ordinary wear and tear explains, and only what is reasonably necessary. Routine turnover cleaning between tenants is generally a cost of running a rental. Check ORS 90.300 or talk to a professional before making deductions.

### When should landlords book cleaning for June turnovers near UO?

Book June turnover cleaning in early spring, as soon as you know which tenants are leaving. Leases around the University of Oregon tend to end close together, so the days after spring term fill quickly. Booking early lets you set a sequence across units and leave a buffer day before each new move-in.
