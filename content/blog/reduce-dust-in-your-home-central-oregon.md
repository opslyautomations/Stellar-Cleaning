If you are looking for how to reduce dust in your house in Central Oregon, the answer is to stop it at the door, trap it instead of moving it, and clean on a steady schedule rather than in bursts. High-desert dust is fine, light and constant, so a routine of mats, damp microfiber, a good vacuum filter and fresh HVAC filters beats any single big clean.

## Why Central Oregon homes get so dusty

Anyone who has moved to Bend, Redmond or Prineville from the valley notices it within a week: a fresh film on the dark furniture, grit on the window sills, and a floor that needs attention far more often than it used to.

A few local conditions stack up:

- **Volcanic soil.** Much of the ground here is ash-derived. The USDA soil survey describes the Deschutes soil series, for example, as an ashy sandy loam formed in volcanic ash on the lava plains of the Deschutes basin, in what it calls the pumice zone. Light, loose soil like that gets tracked in on shoes and paws and lifted by wind. You can read the [USDA description of the Deschutes soil series](https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/D/DESCHUTES.html).
- **A dry climate.** The same USDA description puts mean annual precipitation for that soil at about 10 inches, with hot, dry summers. Less rain means less to hold dust on the ground.
- **Wind.** Wind moves that dry soil around yards, construction sites and unpaved shoulders, and some of it ends up inside.
- **Spring pollen.** Western juniper grows across a large part of central Oregon and sheds its pollen in spring, according to the [USDA Forest Service silvics guide to western juniper](https://research.fs.usda.gov/silvics/western-juniper). That yellow film on cars and patio furniture finds its way indoors too.
- **New construction.** Redmond and Bend both have plenty of recently built homes and nearby building sites, and construction dust settles for months.

None of this is a sign that your house is dirty. It is the environment, and the fix is a routine built for it.

## Stop dust at the door

A lot of the dust in a high-desert home walks or blows in from outside, so the easiest wins happen at the entry.

- **Use two mats at every door.** A coarse scraper mat outside to knock off grit, and an absorbent mat inside to catch what is left. Shake or vacuum them often, or they turn into dust sources themselves.
- **Go shoes-off inside.** A rack and a bench by the door make it easy. Grit that never crosses the threshold never needs vacuuming.
- **Wipe pets' paws.** Keep an old towel by the back door for dogs coming in from a dusty yard.
- **Seal the gaps.** Worn weatherstripping and door sweeps let in a steady trickle of fine dust on windy days. Replacing them is a small job.
- **Keep windows closed on windy afternoons.** Open up in the calm morning or evening instead.

## How to reduce dust in your house with a weekly routine

Once less dust is coming in, the job is to remove what is there without throwing it back into the air.

1. **Dust high to low.** Ceiling fans, tops of door frames and shelves first, then furniture and counters, then baseboards, then floors.
2. **Use damp microfiber, not a feather duster.** A slightly damp cloth traps fine dust. A dry duster just moves it to the next surface.
3. **Vacuum with a HEPA-filter vacuum.** Fine high-desert dust passes straight through a weak filter and back into the room. Go slowly, in overlapping passes, and empty the bin outside.
4. **Mop hard floors after vacuuming.** Vacuuming lifts the grit, mopping takes the film that is left.
5. **Wash bedding weekly.** Beds collect a surprising amount of dust and pollen.
6. **Clean window tracks and sills.** On a windy day, sills are where dust lands first.
7. **Declutter dust traps.** Open shelving, stacked papers and piles of decorative pillows all hold dust. Fewer surfaces means less to clean.

## Air, filters and humidity

Your HVAC system moves all the air in the house, so its filter does a lot of dust control for you.

- **Check the furnace or HVAC filter monthly** during windy, dusty or smoky stretches, and replace it when it looks gray.
- **Use the best filter your system is rated for.** Your system's manual or an HVAC technician can tell you the limit.
- **Consider a portable HEPA air cleaner** for the bedroom or the room where you spend the most time.
- **Watch humidity if you run a humidifier.** Dry air is the norm here, and many people add moisture in winter. The EPA's [mold and moisture guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) recommends keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent, so do not overdo it. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my house so dusty in Bend?

Your house is dusty because Bend's dry climate, light volcanic soil and frequent wind keep fine particles moving, and some of them get inside on shoes, pets and air leaks. Spring pollen and nearby construction add to it. Mats at every door, a shoes-off rule, HEPA vacuuming and regular HVAC filter changes make the biggest difference.

### Does an air purifier help with dust?

Yes, a portable HEPA air purifier helps by pulling fine airborne dust out of the room before it settles. It works best in the rooms you use most, like a bedroom or home office, and it should be sized for the room. It does not remove dust that has already landed, so you still need regular damp dusting and vacuuming.

### How often should I dust in a high-desert climate?

Most Central Oregon homes do best with dusting and vacuuming at least once a week, and more often on high-traffic floors and entryways. Homes with pets, allergies or nearby construction may need floors done twice a week. A recurring professional clean every week or two keeps baseboards, sills and other build-up spots from getting away from you.

### Is it better to dust or vacuum first?

Dust first, then vacuum. Dusting from high surfaces down knocks particles onto the floor, and vacuuming afterward picks them up. Use a damp microfiber cloth so less dust gets into the air, and finish hard floors with a mop to remove the fine film that vacuuming leaves behind.

### Do HVAC filters really make a difference with dust?

Yes, because the HVAC system circulates all the air in your home through its filter. A clogged or low-grade filter lets more fine dust recirculate. Check it monthly during windy, dusty or smoky periods, replace it when it looks gray, and use the highest-rated filter your system is designed to handle.
