A good move-out cleaning checklist for Oregon renters covers every room top to bottom, the inside of every appliance, and the spots landlords check first: ovens, bathroom grout, baseboards and window tracks. Under Oregon law a landlord can only keep deposit money for unpaid rent, other lease defaults and damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, so a thorough clean is the most direct way to protect your deposit. Below is the full checklist, followed by a plain-language summary of the rules.

## What Oregon law says about deposits and cleaning

Oregon's security deposit rules are in [ORS 90.300](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors090.html). The key points for anyone moving out:

- **What a landlord may deduct.** Only amounts reasonably necessary to remedy your defaults under the rental agreement (unpaid rent, for example) and to repair damage you caused, not including ordinary wear and tear.
- **The deadline.** The landlord must return the deposit, or a written accounting that states specifically the basis of any claim, within 31 days after the tenancy ends and you hand back possession.
- **How it arrives.** The accounting goes by personal delivery or first-class mail, or by email if your agreement allows it.
- **If it goes wrong.** If a landlord misses the accounting requirement or withholds money in bad faith, the statute lets the tenant recover twice the amount wrongfully withheld.

Separately, [ORS 90.325](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors090.html) requires tenants to keep the areas they control as clean and sanitary as the condition of the premises permits. In practice that is why a filthy oven or a grease-coated range hood can turn into a deduction, while faded paint or carpet worn thin along a hallway generally cannot.

### The carpet cleaning rule

Carpet is the one place Oregon spells out cleaning conditions. A landlord may deduct carpet cleaning regardless of whether you cleaned it yourself only if all three are true: the cleaning is done with a machine designed for cleaning or shampooing carpets, the carpet was cleaned or replaced after the previous tenant left, and your written rental agreement says the landlord may make that deduction. Read your lease before you move out so you know whether this clause applies to you.

This is a summary, not legal advice. Statutes change, so check the current text of ORS 90.300 or talk to a tenant attorney or legal aid office if you are disputing a deduction.

## The move-out cleaning checklist Oregon landlords expect, starting with the kitchen

The kitchen usually takes the longest, so start there. Work top to bottom so dust and crumbs fall onto surfaces you have not cleaned yet.

- Clear and wipe the inside of every cabinet and drawer, including shelf edges
- Wipe cabinet doors and handles, especially around the stove and trash area
- Clean the oven interior, racks and door glass, and the drip pans or burner grates
- Degrease the range hood and wash or replace the filter
- Empty, defrost if needed, and wipe the refrigerator and freezer, including drawers and door seals
- Pull the fridge and range out if you can and clean the floor and wall behind them
- Run the dishwasher empty, then wipe the door edge and filter area
- Scrub the sink, faucet and backsplash
- Wipe light switches and outlet covers
- Sweep and mop, paying attention to the edges along the cabinet kickboards

## Bathrooms

Soap scum and hard-water spots are easy for a landlord to spot and photograph, so give bathrooms a full pass.

- Scrub the tub, shower walls, grout and fixtures
- Clean the shower door tracks or replace a worn shower curtain liner if you supplied it
- Clean the toilet inside and out, including the base and behind it
- Wipe the mirror, medicine cabinet and vanity inside and out
- Dust the exhaust fan cover
- Wipe towel bars, the toilet paper holder and door handles
- Mop the floor and wipe the baseboards

## Bedrooms, living areas and the rest of the home

These rooms look fine at a glance, which is why the details get skipped.

1. Remove every nail and hook you added, if your lease allows, and note any holes for the landlord rather than guessing at a patch.
2. Dust ceiling fans, light fixtures, blinds and window sills.
3. Clean window glass inside and wipe out the window tracks.
4. Wipe baseboards, door frames, doors and closet shelves.
5. Wipe scuffs and fingerprints from walls only with a method that will not lift the paint. Test a hidden spot first.
6. Vacuum carpets thoroughly, including closet floors and along the edges.
7. Sweep and mop hard floors.
8. Check the entry, laundry area, garage and patio for leftover items and debris.

Last step: take dated photos of every room after you finish. If a deduction comes up later, photos of a clean unit are the clearest record you have.

## What counts as wear and tear versus a cleaning problem

Oregon's statute uses the phrase "ordinary wear and tear" without listing examples, so the line is drawn case by case. A useful way to think about it:

- **Usually wear and tear:** sun-faded paint, light traffic patterns on carpet, minor scuffs where furniture sat, loose door handles from years of use.
- **Usually a cleaning or damage issue:** baked-on grease in the oven, mildew left to build up in the shower, pet hair and odor, food left in the fridge, trash or belongings left behind, stains from spills.

If your landlord gave you a move-in condition report, compare against it. Anything already noted at move-in should not land on you.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does an Oregon landlord have to return a security deposit?

An Oregon landlord has 31 days after the tenancy ends and you return possession to give back the deposit or a written accounting of any deductions, under ORS 90.300. The accounting must state the specific basis of each claim. Leave a forwarding address in writing so the deposit or accounting can reach you. Check the current statute if your situation is unusual.

### Can my landlord charge me for carpet cleaning in Oregon?

Your landlord can deduct carpet cleaning regardless of how clean you leave it only if three conditions are met. The cleaning must use a machine designed for cleaning or shampooing carpets, the carpet must have been cleaned or replaced after the last tenant, and your written rental agreement must allow the deduction. Otherwise, normal carpet wear is not deductible.

### Should the unit be empty before a move-out clean?

Yes, an empty unit gives the best move-out clean because crews can reach baseboards, closet floors and the floor under where furniture stood. Move your belongings out first, then schedule the clean for the last day or two before keys are due. That way nothing gets tracked back in before the landlord's inspection.

### What do landlords look at during a move-out inspection?

Landlords tend to look hardest at the oven, refrigerator, bathroom grout and fixtures, and floors, because those show neglect most clearly. Inside cabinets, window tracks, baseboards and light fixtures come next. Work through the move out cleaning checklist Oregon renters use above, in the same order, and take dated photos when you finish.

### Is a move-out clean the same as a deep clean?

A move-out clean is a deep clean done on an empty home, aimed at returning the unit to move-in condition. It covers the inside of appliances and cabinets, baseboards, window tracks and fixtures that a regular clean skips. The empty rooms make it more thorough, and the deposit inspection gives it a firm deadline.
