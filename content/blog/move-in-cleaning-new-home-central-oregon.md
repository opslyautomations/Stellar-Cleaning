Move-in cleaning in Redmond, Oregon is easiest in the short window between getting the keys and carrying in the first box. Once furniture is in place, the inside of cabinets, the floor under the bed and the top of the fridge get much harder to reach. Whether the house is new to you or newly built, a full clean of an empty home gives you a known starting point. Here is what to cover and why newer homes in Central Oregon need extra attention.

## Why move-in cleaning matters in a growing region

Central Oregon has added a lot of housing in recent years. According to Portland State University's [Population Research Center](https://www.pdx.edu/population-research/population-estimate-reports), Redmond's population grew from about 33,300 in the 2020 Census base to about 38,200 in its July 2025 estimate. Over the same period Bend grew from about 99,300 to about 107,100, and Prineville from about 10,700 to about 12,300.

Growth like that means plenty of newly built homes and plenty of resale homes changing hands. Both need a clean before move-in, for different reasons:

- **A resale home** carries whatever the previous owners or tenants left behind: grease in the range hood, residue inside cabinets, dust on top of door frames and in the vents.
- **A newly built home** may look spotless, but drywall, sawing and sanding leave fine construction dust in places a quick builder's clean does not reach.

## A move-in cleaning checklist for an empty Redmond home

Do this before any furniture or boxes come in. Work top to bottom and room by room.

### Kitchen

- Wipe the inside of every cabinet and drawer before you line or fill them
- Clean the inside of the oven, microwave, refrigerator and freezer
- Degrease the range hood and check the filter
- Run the dishwasher empty and wipe the door edges
- Scrub the sink and faucet and wipe the countertops and backsplash

### Bathrooms

- Clean and sanitize the toilet, tub, shower and sink
- Wipe inside the vanity and medicine cabinet
- Dust the exhaust fan cover
- Mop the floor and wipe the baseboards

### Every room

1. Dust ceiling fans, light fixtures and the tops of door frames.
2. Wipe window sills and clean out window tracks.
3. Clean interior window glass.
4. Wipe baseboards, doors, switch plates and outlet covers.
5. Wipe closet shelves and rods.
6. Vacuum carpets thoroughly, including closet floors.
7. Sweep and mop hard floors last.

Tip: if you are painting, refinishing or replacing flooring, do that work first and clean last, so the dust from the project does not end up in your clean cabinets.

## Construction dust in newly built homes

Construction dust is the big difference between moving into a new build and moving into a resale home. It is fine, it travels, and it keeps appearing for a while after the trades leave. Newer buildings are not automatically easier to clean. Dust can keep settling for months, and modern finishes on counters, floors and fixtures are less forgiving of the wrong product than older surfaces.

What helps:

- **Clean in more than one pass.** A first pass removes the bulk of the dust, and a second pass catches what settled afterward.
- **Start high.** Fans, light fixtures, the tops of cabinets and door frames hold dust that will drift down onto anything you cleaned below.
- **Get into the tracks.** Window tracks, sliding door tracks and cabinet hinges collect grit.
- **Use the right product for the finish.** Check the care instructions for new countertops and flooring before using anything abrasive or acidic.
- **Let in fresh air.** New building materials and furnishings can give off volatile organic compounds. The EPA's guidance on [indoor air and VOCs](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality) recommends increasing ventilation, so open windows when the weather allows.

Ask your builder whether the HVAC filter was changed after construction ended. If not, changing it is a quick win. Duct cleaning, if you want it, is a specialist job.

## Moving into a resale home in Bend, Redmond or Prineville

A previously lived-in home needs a deeper clean in different places. Focus on the parts previous occupants were least likely to clean before leaving:

- Inside the oven and under the stovetop burners
- Behind and under the refrigerator and range, if you can move them
- Inside bathroom vanities and around the base of toilets
- Light fixtures, ceiling fans and the tops of upper cabinets
- Window tracks, baseboards and closet floors

Central Oregon's dry climate also means fine dust builds up on sills and in tracks quickly, so expect more of it there than you might in a valley home.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the best time for move in cleaning in Redmond, Oregon?

The best time for move in cleaning in Redmond, Oregon is after you get the keys and before any furniture or boxes arrive. An empty home lets you clean inside every cabinet, behind appliances and along every baseboard. If you are painting or doing flooring work, finish that first, then clean, then move in.

### Does a newly built home really need cleaning before move-in?

Yes, a newly built home usually needs a thorough clean before move-in because construction leaves fine dust on surfaces, in cabinets and in window tracks. A builder's clean often focuses on the visible surfaces. Dust can also keep settling for weeks or months, so plan for more than one pass in a new build.

### What is the difference between move-in cleaning and post-construction cleaning?

Move-in cleaning prepares a lived-in or lightly dusty home for new occupants, focusing on kitchens, bathrooms, cabinets and floors. Post-construction cleaning removes the heavy dust and debris left by building or remodeling work, usually in several passes. A brand-new home with a lot of builder dust may need post-construction cleaning first.

### Should I clean before or after painting a new home?

Clean after painting. Sanding, patching and painting create dust and drips that land on floors, sills and counters. Finish the paint and any flooring work first, let the dust settle, then do the full move-in clean. Open windows during and after painting to help clear fumes.

### How do I deal with dust in a new Central Oregon home?

Clean from the top down in more than one pass, starting with fans, fixtures and the tops of cabinets and door frames. Wipe window sills and tracks often, since Central Oregon's dry climate and construction dust both collect there. Check whether your HVAC filter was replaced after construction and change it if not.
