Post-construction cleaning for Redmond and Bend projects is a multi-pass job, not a single visit: a rough clean while trades are finishing, a detailed final clean once work is done, and a touch-up right before handover or move-in. Fine construction dust keeps settling for days after the last tool is packed, so one clean almost never holds. Planning for all three phases is what gets a space ready on the date you promised.

## Why post-construction cleaning in Redmond takes several passes

Drywall sanding, cutting and finishing produce dust fine enough to hang in the air and drift into every room. It lands on ledges, inside cabinets, on light fixtures and in window tracks, then lands again after you wipe it. Central Oregon's dry air does not help. In Bend and Redmond, fine dust is a year-round condition rather than a seasonal one, and a new build adds a heavy layer on top of it.

Newer buildings are also less forgiving than people expect. Modern counters, flooring and fixtures can be dulled or etched by the wrong product or a gritty cloth, so construction cleanup is as much about method as effort. That is why post construction cleaning Redmond projects need is quoted as a multi-pass job from the start, rather than discovered halfway through.

## The three phases of construction cleanup

### Rough clean

The rough clean happens while finish trades are still on site, often after drywall and before flooring, paint touch-ups or fixtures are complete. The goal is to stop dust and debris building up underfoot and in finished areas.

- Loose debris, packaging and scraps gathered for the contractor's disposal
- Floors swept and vacuumed
- Heavy dust removed from window sills, ledges and rough openings
- Bathrooms and kitchens cleared enough for trades to work cleanly

### Final clean

The final clean is the detailed, top-to-bottom pass after construction work is finished. It is the biggest of the three and the one most people picture.

1. Dust walls, ceiling corners, vents and light fixtures from the top down
2. Remove stickers, labels and protective film from windows, fixtures and appliances
3. Clean windows, frames, sills and tracks inside
4. Wipe the inside and outside of cabinets, drawers and closets
5. Clean appliances inside and out
6. Clean and polish sinks, tubs, showers, toilets and fixtures
7. Wipe doors, door frames, switch plates and baseboards
8. Vacuum and wash hard floors, and vacuum carpets thoroughly

Sticker and label removal deserves its own line on a quote. Window manufacturer labels, appliance stickers and film on fixtures take time, and scraping the wrong way leaves scratches on glass or finishes.

### Touch-up clean

The touch-up clean happens just before handover, final walkthrough or move-in. It catches dust that has resettled since the final clean, fingerprints from late punch-list work, and floors tracked on by the last trades through. It is short, but it is the clean your client or buyer actually sees.

## Timing cleanup with contractors and inspections

The most common scheduling mistake is booking the final clean before the trades are done. If a painter, electrician or flooring installer is still coming back, the space will need cleaning again.

A workable sequence:

1. Rough clean after the dustiest trades, typically drywall and sanding
2. Final clean once punch-list work is complete and the trades are off site
3. Touch-up clean a day or two before the final walkthrough, handover or move-in

Final building inspections are about code compliance, not cleanliness, but a clean site makes the walkthrough easier for everyone. In Redmond and Bend, coordinate cleaning dates with your general contractor or site superintendent so the crew is not working around active trades. Give the cleaning company as much notice as you can, because slots around month-end handovers fill quickly.

## Dust, safety and the jobs that need a specialist

How dust gets cleaned matters, not just whether it gets cleaned. Federal OSHA's construction silica standard, [29 CFR 1926.1153](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1153), which Oregon OSHA has adopted, restricts dry sweeping and dry brushing where they could contribute to worker exposure to respirable crystalline silica, unless wet sweeping, HEPA-filtered vacuuming or other low-dust methods are not feasible. It also restricts using compressed air to clean surfaces. That is a rule for employers on construction sites, but the logic carries over: wet methods and HEPA vacuums beat brooms and blowers for construction dust.

Some jobs are outside general cleaning, and we will say so plainly:

- **Pre-1978 remodels.** Renovations that disturb painted surfaces in older homes and child-occupied facilities can fall under lead-safe work rules, including a cleaning verification step that a certified renovator must perform. The Oregon Health Authority explains these [rules for renovating pre-1978 buildings](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/PH/HealthyEnvironments/HealthyNeighborhoods/LeadPoisoning/ChildCareSchools/Pages/RulesforRenovating.aspx). Check with your contractor or OHA if your project qualifies.
- **HVAC ducts.** Construction dust in ductwork is a job for an HVAC contractor. Ask whoever installed the system about filters and duct cleaning.
- **Carpet extraction.** We vacuum carpets thoroughly. Hot-water extraction or shampooing is specialist work.
- **Debris hauling.** Dumpsters and heavy material removal belong to the contractor.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check the current rules or talk to a professional about your specific project.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should post-construction cleaning be scheduled?

Schedule the final clean after all trades have finished, including punch-list work, and the touch-up clean a day or two before handover, final walkthrough or move-in. A rough clean can happen earlier, usually after drywall and sanding. Booking the final clean while trades are still returning means paying for the space to be cleaned twice.

### Why does dust keep coming back after a construction clean?

Fine construction dust stays airborne for a long time and keeps settling for days after work stops. It also hides in vents, tracks and cabinets and gets disturbed by foot traffic. That is why construction cleanup is done in phases, with a touch-up pass just before handover. In Central Oregon's dry climate, ordinary fine dust adds to the problem.

### Is post-construction cleaning the builder's job or the owner's?

It depends on the contract. Many general contractors include a rough clean and leave the detailed final clean to the owner, while others include a final clean in their scope. Read your construction contract or ask your builder what is included, then fill the gap so the space is truly ready for occupancy.

### How do you remove construction stickers without scratching glass?

Construction stickers and labels come off safely with the right solvent, patience and a fresh blade held at a low angle on glass, never on coated or tinted film. Trying to scrape them off dry or with an old blade is how glass and finishes get scratched. Plan for label removal as a separate task, because a new build can have dozens of them.

### Can you clean while contractors are still working?

Yes, a rough clean is designed to happen while finish trades are still on site, to keep dust and debris under control. The detailed final clean should wait until the trades are done, because ongoing work creates new dust and marks. Coordinate dates with your general contractor so the crew is not working around active trades.
