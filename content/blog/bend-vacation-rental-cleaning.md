Bend vacation rental cleaning is a deadline job: the last guest checks out, the next one checks in a few hours later, and everything in between has to be cleaned, reset and checked against a standard a paying visitor will judge. It works best with a written turnover checklist, plan for Central Oregon's dust, and keep their permits and posted information current. Here is how to put that together.

## Bend vacation rental cleaning starts with the permit basics

Before you think about cleaning schedules, make sure the rental itself is legal to operate. According to the City of Bend's [short-term rental permits and licenses page](https://bendoregon.gov/services/business/short-term-rentals/):

- Most short-term rentals in city limits need a **land use permit** and an active **operating license**. A few resort areas are exempt from the land use permit, so check how your property is treated.
- Hosts submit a **fire safety self-inspection checklist** with the license application and each renewal.
- Hosts provide a **24/7 emergency contact** and notify neighbors within 250 feet.
- **Good Neighbor Guidelines** must be posted inside the rental or in your online listing.
- The operating license is **renewed annually**, with proof the property was used.

If your property is outside city limits in unincorporated Deschutes County, the county requires short-term rentals to register and collect transient room tax. Details are on the [Deschutes County transient room tax page](https://deschutescounty.gov/finance/page/transient-room-tax). Redmond rentals inside city limits fall under the City of Redmond's own rules, so check with the city directly. Requirements change, so confirm the current rules with the city or county rather than relying on a summary.

The cleaning connection: your turnover is the moment you confirm the posted guidelines are still in place, the emergency contact information is visible, and the smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are present and undamaged.

## A guest-ready turnover checklist

Use the same Bend vacation rental cleaning list on every turnover, whether you clean it yourself or hand it off. Consistency is what keeps reviews steady.

### Kitchen

- Load and run the dishwasher, then put away clean dishes in their marked spots
- Wipe counters, backsplash, cabinet fronts and handles
- Clean the stovetop, microwave inside and out, and the coffee maker
- Clear the refrigerator of anything guests left and wipe the shelves
- Empty trash and recycling and replace liners
- Sweep and mop the floor

### Bathrooms

- Clean and sanitize the toilet, sink, tub and shower
- Check the shower and tub drains for hair
- Polish mirrors and fixtures
- Replace towels and bath mats
- Mop the floor

### Bedrooms and living areas

- Strip every bed, including unused ones guests may have napped on
- Make beds with fresh linens to your standard
- Dust surfaces, lamps, headboards and window sills
- Wipe high-touch points: remotes, light switches, door handles, thermostats
- Vacuum carpets and rugs, including under beds
- Check under furniture and cushions for left-behind items

### Final reset and inspection

1. Walk every room against your listing photos and reset furniture to match.
2. Check for damage, stains or broken items and photograph anything you find.
3. Confirm Wi-Fi details, house rules and Good Neighbor Guidelines are in place.
4. Set the thermostat and lights for arrival.
5. Lock up and confirm the door code or lockbox is reset.

## Linens and restocking: decide who owns them

Plenty of turnovers go wrong at the handoffs rather than the cleaning itself. Decide in writing who is responsible for each of these, whether that is you, your cleaner or a separate linen service:

- Laundering and storing linens and towels, with at least one spare set per bed on site
- Restocking toilet paper, paper towels, soap, dish detergent, trash bags and coffee
- Reporting low supplies before they run out
- Reporting maintenance issues, from a dripping faucet to a burned-out bulb

If you work with a cleaning company, settle these at the walkthrough so the scope is clear before the first booking.

## Dust, tracked-in needles and the high-desert factor

Bend's climate means fine dust is a constant rather than a seasonal problem. It settles on window sills, ceiling fans, blinds and dark furniture fast, and guests notice it on surfaces they touch. Build it into every turnover:

- Dust top to bottom so you are not re-dusting cleaned surfaces
- Wipe window sills and tracks at every turnover in dry months
- Vacuum entry rugs thoroughly, where tracked-in grit, pine needles and trail dust collect
- Put a sturdy mat at each entry and shake it out at each turnover
- Run a microfiber cloth over dark furniture and electronics, where dust shows most

If your rental has a hot tub, water care, chemical balancing and equipment checks are specialist work. Use a hot tub service technician for that. A cleaning crew should not be adjusting chemicals.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a Bend vacation rental turnover take?

A Bend vacation rental turnover usually takes a few hours for a small home and longer for larger properties with several bedrooms and bathrooms. The time depends on the size of the rental, how the last guests left it, and how much laundry and restocking is in scope. Plan the gap between checkout and check-in around the largest homes you run.

### Do I need a permit to run a short-term rental in Bend?

Yes. Most short-term rentals in Bend city limits need both a land use permit and an operating license from the City of Bend, renewed each year, with a few resort-area exceptions for the land use permit. Rentals in unincorporated Deschutes County register with the county instead. Confirm current requirements with the city or county before you list.

### Who should handle linens for a vacation rental?

Linens should be handled by whoever you assign in writing: you, your cleaner or a dedicated linen service. The key is that someone owns it, with at least one spare set per bed on site so a turnover never waits on a dryer. Agree on the arrangement before the first booking.

### Should my vacation rental cleaner also service the hot tub?

No. Hot tub water care, chemical balancing and equipment checks belong with a hot tub service technician, not a general cleaning crew. Your turnover checklist can include a visual check that the cover is secured and nothing looks wrong, with any issue reported to you and your hot tub technician right away.

### How do I keep dust down in a Bend rental between guests?

Dust top to bottom at every turnover, wipe window sills and tracks, and vacuum entry rugs where grit and pine needles collect. Use sturdy mats at every entry and shake them out each time. In dry months, give dark furniture and electronics an extra pass, since fine high-desert dust shows there first.
