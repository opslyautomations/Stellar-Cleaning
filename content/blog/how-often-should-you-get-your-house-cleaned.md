How often should you get your house cleaned? For most households, bi-weekly professional cleaning is the sweet spot, weekly makes sense if you have pets, young kids, allergies or a lot of foot traffic, and monthly works for small, quiet homes where you keep up between visits. The right answer depends on who lives in the house and what the weather is tracking in.

## How often should you get your house cleaned? The short answer

Here is how the three common schedules compare:

- **Weekly** keeps dust, pet hair and kitchen and bathroom grime from building up at all. Best for busy, full households.
- **Bi-weekly** (every two weeks) handles most homes well, with light tidying from you in between.
- **Monthly** suits small homes, single occupants or couples without pets who already clean regularly and want a professional reset.

Whichever you choose, start with a deep clean if the home has not had a thorough clean in a while. Regular visits are designed to maintain, not to catch up. Our guide to [deep cleaning vs regular cleaning](/blog/deep-cleaning-vs-regular-house-cleaning) explains the difference.

## Weekly cleaning: who it suits

Weekly cleaning is the right call when the house gets dirty faster than you can keep up. That usually means one or more of these:

- Shedding dogs or cats, especially more than one
- Babies, toddlers or school-age kids
- Someone in the home with allergies or asthma
- Two working adults with long commutes and little time on weekdays
- A home office or a lot of daily cooking

Weekly also has a practical advantage: every visit is a light one, because nothing has time to build up. Soap scum, grease and pet hair are all easier to remove after seven days than after thirty.

## Bi-weekly cleaning: the most common choice

Bi-weekly is where most households end up. It keeps kitchens and bathrooms consistently clean and floors under control, while leaving room in the budget and the calendar. It works best if you handle light daily tasks yourself, such as dishes, wiping counters after cooking and a quick vacuum of high-traffic areas if you have a pet.

If you are unsure how often you should get your house cleaned, bi-weekly is a sensible starting point. You can move to weekly if the second week starts to feel noticeably dirty, or to monthly if the home stays in good shape.

## Monthly cleaning: when it is enough

Monthly cleaning suits a small home with few occupants, no pets and good daily habits. It gives you a professional reset of bathrooms, kitchen and floors once a month while you handle the basics in between.

The trade-off is that each visit has more to undo, so a monthly clean is closer in effort to a light deep clean. If you find yourself scrubbing between visits, a bi-weekly schedule is probably the better fit.

## Pets, kids and allergies change the answer

Allergies and asthma are the strongest reason to clean more often. The [EPA's guidance on asthma triggers](https://www.epa.gov/asthma/asthma-triggers-gain-control) recommends vacuuming carpets and furniture every week to control dust mites and washing bedding in hot water weekly. It also suggests vacuuming when the person with asthma is not around. A weekly professional clean, scheduled while you are out, fits that guidance well.

Pets add hair and dander that settle on floors, upholstery and baseboards and come back within days. Kids add fingerprints, spills and floor clutter. Neither means you need daily cleaning, but both push most households from monthly toward bi-weekly, or from bi-weekly toward weekly.

## Wet Willamette Valley winters and tracked-in mud

The season matters as much as the household. According to the [National Weather Service's Eugene climate summary](https://www.weather.gov/media/pqr/climate/ClimateBookEugene/pg1.pdf), nearly 90 percent of the area's annual rainfall falls between early October and mid-May. Albany and Lebanon share the same wet-winter pattern, which means months of wet shoes, muddy paws and grit dragged across entryways and hard floors.

A few ways to plan around it:

1. Consider moving up one step in frequency from roughly October through May, for example from bi-weekly to weekly, then back again for summer.
2. Put a sturdy mat outside every door and another inside, and keep a shoe spot near the entry.
3. Ask for entryways, mudrooms and the floors closest to the doors to be a standing priority on every visit.
4. Book a deep clean in spring, once the wettest months have passed, to reset floors, baseboards and window tracks.

Grit on hard floors is not just a looks problem. It scratches finishes over time, so keeping it under control in the wet season protects the floor as well. For more on that, see our post on [rainy season floor care in the Willamette Valley](/blog/rainy-season-floor-care-willamette-valley).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is bi-weekly house cleaning enough?

Bi-weekly house cleaning is enough for most households. It keeps kitchens, bathrooms and floors consistently clean as long as you handle light daily tasks, such as dishes and wiping counters, between visits. Homes with several pets, young children, allergies or heavy foot traffic often find the second week gets noticeably dirty, and weekly service suits them better.

### How often should you clean your house if you have pets?

If you have pets, weekly cleaning is usually the best fit, and bi-weekly is the minimum for most homes with a dog or cat. Pet hair and dander settle on floors, furniture and baseboards and return within days. Homes with several animals, heavy shedders or someone with allergies benefit most from a weekly schedule focused on floors and upholstery.

### Should I clean more often in the rainy season?

Yes, many Willamette Valley homes benefit from cleaning more often during the rainy months. Most of the region's yearly rain falls between October and May, which means more mud and grit tracked across entryways and hard floors. Moving up one step in frequency for those months, such as from bi-weekly to weekly, keeps floors from getting ahead of you.

### Is monthly house cleaning worth it?

Monthly house cleaning is worth it for small homes with few occupants and no pets, where daily habits keep things in reasonable shape. It gives you a professional reset of kitchens, bathrooms and floors once a month. Because more builds up between visits, each monthly clean takes longer, and busier households usually get better results from a bi-weekly schedule.

### Can I change my cleaning schedule later?

Yes. A cleaning schedule should change when your household does, whether that is a new pet, a new baby, guests for the holidays or the start of the rainy season. Most people start bi-weekly and adjust after a few visits. Let us know what is changing and we will recommend a frequency that fits your home as it is now.
