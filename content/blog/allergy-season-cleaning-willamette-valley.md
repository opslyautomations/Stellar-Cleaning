The most useful allergy season cleaning tips all do one of two things: keep pollen from getting inside, or remove it from the places it settles before it gets stirred back up. In the Willamette Valley that means entryways, bedding, floors and air filters, cleaned more often from late spring into early summer when grass pollen runs high.

## Why the Willamette Valley is tough on allergies

The valley grows a lot of grass. Oregon State University's field crops program describes the Willamette Valley as the grass seed capital of the world, and notes that it produces almost two-thirds of the total cool-season grass seed grown in the United States. You can read more on [OSU's Willamette Valley grass seed page](https://valleyfieldcrops.oregonstate.edu/grass-seed).

Add lawns, pastures and roadsides, and grass pollen season is a big deal here. A local allergist told OPB that grass pollen season in the valley typically runs from late May to July. Read the [OPB report on the Willamette Valley allergy season](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/05/08/allergy-season-is-accelerating-in-the-willamette-valley/) for more on how weather affects it year to year.

Tree pollen comes earlier in spring, so for many people in Salem, Albany, Corvallis and Eugene the sneezing starts well before grass season and carries through it. Cleaning will not replace medical care. If your symptoms are severe, talk to your doctor or an allergist. What cleaning can do is make your home a place where you get a break from pollen.

## How pollen gets into your home

Pollen does not need an open window to get inside, although that helps it. It comes in on:

- **Clothes, hair and shoes** after time outdoors
- **Pets**, especially dogs who roll in the grass
- **Open windows and doors** on warm, breezy days
- **Air leaks** around doors, windows and vents
- **Grocery bags, backpacks and outdoor gear** set down on furniture

Once inside, it settles on floors, beds, upholstery, curtains and every flat surface, and it goes airborne again every time someone walks across the rug or flops onto the couch.

## Allergy season cleaning tips for every room

### Entryways

1. Put a mat outside and inside every door you use.
2. Take shoes off at the door and keep them there.
3. Have a spot near the entry for jackets and bags that have been outside.
4. Wipe dogs down with a damp towel when they come in.
5. Vacuum or shake out entry mats often. They fill up fast in spring.

### Bedrooms

- **Wash bedding weekly** in the hottest water the fabric care label allows. Pillowcases matter most, since your face is on them all night.
- **Shower and change before bed** on high-pollen days, so you are not carrying pollen into the sheets.
- **Keep pets off the bed** during peak season if you can manage it.
- **Keep bedroom windows closed** during the day when pollen counts are high.
- **Cut the clutter.** Stacks of clothes, piles of pillows and open shelving all hold dust and pollen.

### Floors and furniture

- **Vacuum with a HEPA-filter vacuum** at least weekly, and more often on rugs and carpet near entries. A weak filter blows fine particles back into the room.
- **Mop hard floors** after vacuuming to pick up what is left.
- **Vacuum upholstered furniture** and the underside of cushions.
- **Damp-dust surfaces** with microfiber instead of a dry duster, which just moves pollen around.
- **Wash throw blankets and decorative pillow covers** along with the bedding.

### Air and windows

- **Replace your HVAC filter** at the start of the season and check it monthly. Use the highest-rated filter your system is designed for.
- **Run a portable HEPA air cleaner** in the bedroom.
- **Wipe window sills and tracks.** Pollen collects there, especially on windows that were open.
- **Wash or vacuum curtains** a couple of times through the season.

## A spring cleaning checklist built for allergy season

The best time for a big reset is right before grass season, so your home starts clean. A deep spring clean should cover:

1. Ceiling fan blades, light fixtures and the tops of door frames
2. Blinds, curtains and window tracks
3. Baseboards, vents and return grilles
4. Behind and under furniture, including beds and couches
5. Mattress and upholstery vacuuming
6. Closet floors and shelves
7. Entry mats replaced or deep cleaned
8. A fresh HVAC filter
9. Pet beds washed
10. Bathroom and kitchen surfaces, including exhaust fan covers

After that, a regular weekly or bi-weekly clean keeps pollen from building back up through the season. Our guide on [how often you should get your house cleaned](/blog/how-often-should-you-get-your-house-cleaned) can help you pick a rhythm.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is grass pollen season in the Willamette Valley?

Grass pollen season in the Willamette Valley typically runs from late May to July, according to a local allergist interviewed by OPB. Exact timing shifts from year to year with the weather, and warm, dry spells can push counts higher. Tree pollen arrives earlier in spring, so many people with allergies notice symptoms well before grass season begins.

### Does vacuuming make allergies worse?

Vacuuming can briefly make allergies worse if the vacuum lets fine particles escape back into the air. A vacuum with a HEPA filter traps much more of the pollen and dust it picks up. If you have allergies, consider having someone else vacuum, or wear a mask while you do it, and leave the room aired out afterward if outdoor pollen counts are low.

### How often should I wash bedding during allergy season?

Wash bedding at least once a week during allergy season, in the hottest water the fabric care label allows. Pillowcases collect the most pollen because they are against your face and hair all night, so some people swap them more often. Showering before bed on high-pollen days also keeps less pollen from reaching your sheets.

### Should I keep my windows closed during grass pollen season?

Yes, keeping windows closed on high-pollen days is one of the simplest ways to keep pollen out of your home. Pollen counts tend to be higher on warm, dry, breezy days. Use your HVAC system with a good filter or a portable HEPA air cleaner to keep air moving, and open up on cool, calm days if you need fresh air.

### What should a spring cleaning include for allergies?

A spring clean for allergies should focus on the places pollen and dust collect and stay. That means blinds, curtains, window tracks, ceiling fans, vents, baseboards, under and behind furniture, mattresses and upholstery, plus a fresh HVAC filter and clean entry mats. Doing it just before grass season gives you the cleanest possible starting point.
