How often should an office be cleaned? Most staffed offices need restrooms, break rooms and trash handled daily or close to it, with full-office cleaning anywhere from once a week to every night depending on headcount and foot traffic. The right answer for your space comes from who uses it, how hard they use it, and what time of year it is.

## The short answer: frequency follows people, not square footage

A common mistake is to set an office cleaning schedule by size alone. A 4,000-square-foot suite with six accountants creates far less mess than a 2,000-square-foot clinic front desk that sees a steady stream of visitors. What drives cleaning frequency is use: how many people are in the space, how many visitors come through, and how much eating, drinking and handwashing happens on site.

Use these rough tiers as a starting point:

- **Small professional suite, under about ten people, little client traffic** — a thorough weekly clean is often enough, with staff handling small spills and kitchen wipe-downs in between.
- **Mid-size office, shared kitchen, regular visitors** — two to three visits a week, with restrooms and break rooms covered on every visit.
- **Busy or client-facing office, larger headcount, multiple restrooms** — nightly service, five days a week, plus attention to entrances and restrooms during the day if traffic is heavy.
- **Shift-based or always-open operations** — cleaning scheduled around your actual operating hours, which may mean overnight or early-morning visits.

These are planning tiers, not rules. The right office cleaning frequency for your building is the one that keeps restrooms stocked, trash from piling up, and floors safe between visits.

## How often should an office be cleaned, area by area?

Not every room needs the same schedule. Splitting the office into zones lets you put the frequency where it matters and avoid paying for attention a room does not need.

### Restrooms

Restrooms are the one area where daily service is close to non-negotiable in a staffed office. Toilets, sinks, faucets, dispensers and floors get used constantly, and an empty paper towel dispenser is the fastest way for an office to feel neglected. Workplace sanitation rules back this up: the federal OSHA sanitation standard, which [Oregon OSHA adopts by reference](https://osha.oregon.gov/rules/final/Pages/division-2.aspx), says places of employment must be kept clean "to the extent that the nature of the work allows" and that waste must be removed as often as necessary to keep the workplace sanitary ([29 CFR 1910.141](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.141)). It does not set a fixed number of cleanings, so read the rule yourself or talk to a professional if you have a compliance question.

### Break rooms and kitchens

Counters, sinks, microwave handles, coffee stations and tables should be cleaned on every visit. The refrigerator is the classic problem: set a weekly clear-out day for staff and have the interior wiped down on a regular cycle so it never becomes a project.

### Workstations and high-touch points

The CDC's facility guidance lists door handles, elevator buttons, touchpads, stair rails, restroom fixtures and desks as high-touch surfaces and recommends cleaning them regularly, adding that high-traffic spaces may need more frequent cleaning or disinfection ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/when-and-how-to-clean-and-disinfect-a-facility.html)). In practice that means door hardware, light switches and shared equipment get wiped on every visit, while cleared desk surfaces are cleaned on a set schedule.

### Floors

Entrances and main walkways take the most dirt, especially during the wet months in the Willamette Valley when everyone tracks water and grit in from the parking lot. Vacuum and mop high-traffic paths on every visit, and lower-use private offices less often.

## Daily vs weekly office cleaning tasks

If you are writing a scope of work, it helps to sort tasks by how often they need to happen. For a full breakdown, see our [office cleaning checklist](/blog/office-cleaning-checklist).

**Daily (or every visit):**

1. Empty trash and recycling, replace liners.
2. Clean and restock restrooms.
3. Wipe break room counters, sinks, tables and appliance exteriors.
4. Disinfect high-touch points: door handles, light switches, shared phones and keyboards where appropriate.
5. Vacuum and spot-mop entrances and main traffic lanes.

**Weekly:**

1. Vacuum all carpeted areas, including private offices.
2. Mop all hard floors.
3. Dust reachable surfaces, sills and ledges.
4. Clean interior glass and partition windows.
5. Wipe cleared desk surfaces.

**Monthly or quarterly:**

1. Detail baseboards, vents and high dusting.
2. Clean inside the break room refrigerator and microwave.
3. Deep clean restroom grout, partitions and fixtures.
4. Review floors for restorative care.

## Adjusting for flu season and busy periods

Cleaning frequency should not be fixed year round. The CDC notes that [flu season in the US usually runs through fall and winter](https://www.cdc.gov/flu/about/season.html), with activity most often peaking between December and February. That is a reasonable window to add a visit, increase high-touch disinfection, or ask your cleaning crew to pay extra attention to shared kitchens and restrooms.

Other times to temporarily step up frequency:

- A visible illness going around the office.
- Year-end client traffic, open houses or audits.
- Weeks with heavy rain, when entrance floors take a beating.
- After a remodel, move-in or furniture delivery.

A good provider will talk through seasonal adjustments at the start of the contract rather than waiting for you to ask.

## Signs your current schedule is not enough

You may need more frequent visits if you notice:

- Restroom supplies running out between cleanings.
- Overflowing bins by mid-week.
- Sticky break room counters or a fridge nobody wants to open.
- Dust building up on sills and monitors.
- Staff quietly cleaning their own areas.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should office restrooms be cleaned?

Office restrooms should be cleaned and restocked daily in any staffed office, and more than once a day in busy or client-facing buildings. Daily service covers toilets, sinks, faucets, mirrors, dispensers, trash and floors. Deeper tasks such as scrubbing grout, cleaning partitions and detailing vents can be handled weekly or monthly. If supplies run out between visits, that is a clear sign your restroom schedule needs more frequency.

### Is weekly cleaning enough for a small office?

Weekly cleaning is often enough for a small office with under about ten people and little client traffic, as long as staff handle minor spills and kitchen wipe-downs between visits. Once you add a shared kitchen, regular visitors or more than one restroom, two or three visits a week usually works better. The deciding factor is how quickly trash, restroom supplies and floors fall behind between cleanings.

### How often should office carpets be vacuumed?

Office carpets in entrances and main walkways should be vacuumed on every cleaning visit, while lower-traffic private offices can usually be vacuumed weekly. Wet weather in the Willamette Valley means entry carpet collects grit quickly, so walk-off mats and frequent vacuuming there protect the rest of the floor. Periodic hot-water carpet extraction is a separate specialist service from routine vacuuming.

### Should offices increase cleaning during flu season?

Yes, many offices increase cleaning during flu season, which the CDC says usually runs through fall and winter and most often peaks between December and February. Adding a visit or increasing disinfection of high-touch points such as door handles, light switches, shared equipment and break room surfaces is a practical step. Always follow the disinfectant label, including how long the surface must stay wet.

### How often should an office get a deep clean?

Most offices benefit from a deep clean every quarter, with some tasks folded into a monthly rotation. A deep clean covers what routine visits skip: baseboards, vents, high dusting, inside appliances, restroom grout and detailed floor care. Busy offices may need it more often. Scheduling deep-clean tasks on a rotation within a recurring contract keeps the office from ever needing a large catch-up job.
