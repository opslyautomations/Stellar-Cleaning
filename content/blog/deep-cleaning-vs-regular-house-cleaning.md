The difference in deep cleaning vs regular cleaning is the difference between resetting a home and maintaining it. A regular clean keeps the surfaces you use every day clean on a schedule. A deep clean reaches everything a regular clean skips, such as inside appliances, behind furniture, baseboards and window tracks, and it is usually how a home starts before it moves onto a regular schedule.

## Deep cleaning vs regular cleaning at a glance

Think of a regular clean as upkeep and a deep clean as a reset. A regular clean assumes the home is already in reasonable shape and keeps it there. A deep clean assumes buildup has formed and removes it, so that regular cleaning can actually keep up afterward.

The two are not competitors. In most homes in Albany and Corvallis, the pattern that works is one deep clean first, then regular visits, then another deep clean once or twice a year, often in spring or before the holidays.

## What a regular house cleaning includes

A regular clean, sometimes called a maintenance or standard clean, focuses on the rooms and surfaces that get daily use. A typical checklist looks like this:

- Dusting reachable surfaces, shelves and decor
- Wiping kitchen counters, sink, stovetop and appliance exteriors
- Cleaning and sanitizing toilets, tubs, showers, sinks and mirrors
- Vacuuming carpets and rugs, and mopping hard floors
- Emptying trash and replacing liners
- Wiping light switches, door handles and other high-touch spots
- Making beds or changing linens if you leave fresh ones out

A regular clean is efficient because it is repeated. Buildup never gets a chance to form, so each visit stays predictable.

## What a deep cleaning includes

A deep clean covers everything above and then goes into the places that collect grime slowly over months. Expect a checklist closer to this:

- Baseboards, door frames, doors and trim wiped by hand
- Window sills and window tracks
- Inside the oven, microwave and refrigerator
- Cabinet fronts degreased, especially near the stove
- Behind and under furniture and appliances that can be moved safely
- Blinds, ceiling fans, light fixtures and vents dusted
- Soap scum and hard-water buildup worked off tile, glass and fixtures
- Grout scrubbed and edges of floors detailed

Deep cleaning also takes more care with products. Disinfectants only work as labeled when the surface is cleaned first and the product stays wet for its full contact time, a point the [EPA's six steps for safe and effective disinfectant use](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-04/documents/disinfectants-onepager.pdf) makes directly. A rushed wipe skips that, which is one reason deep cleans take longer.

## When you need a deep clean first

Start with a deep clean when any of these apply:

1. It has been more than a couple of months since the home had a thorough clean.
2. You are starting professional cleaning for the first time.
3. You are moving in or moving out. See our [move-out cleaning checklist for Oregon](/blog/move-out-cleaning-checklist-oregon) for that specific case.
4. You have had guests, a renovation, a new pet or a long illness in the house.
5. The wet season has been tracking mud and grit through entryways for months.

Starting with a deep clean is not an upsell. A regular clean on top of months of buildup cannot do its job, because time that should go to maintenance goes to scrubbing instead. Our residential service page puts it plainly: most clients start with a deep clean and then move onto a regular schedule.

One caution. Recurring black spots on a bathroom ceiling or around a window are a moisture sign, not just a cleaning task. The [EPA's brief guide to mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) notes that more ventilation and more frequent cleaning usually keep small bathroom mold in check, but larger areas or water damage may need a specialist. ## How often to schedule each type

For regular cleaning, most homes land on weekly or bi-weekly. Weekly suits households with pets, young kids, allergies or a lot of foot traffic. Bi-weekly suits most other homes. Monthly works for small, low-traffic homes but leaves more buildup between visits. Our guide on [how often you should get your house cleaned](/blog/how-often-should-you-get-your-house-cleaned) goes deeper into each option.

For deep cleaning, once or twice a year is typical on top of a regular schedule. Many people time it for spring, after the wettest months, or before hosting family over the holidays. If you are not on a regular schedule, a deep clean every few months keeps things from slipping too far.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a deep clean worth it before starting regular cleaning?

Yes, in most homes a deep clean is worth doing first. It removes buildup on baseboards, cabinet fronts, appliances, grout and window tracks that a regular clean is not designed to tackle. Once that reset is done, regular visits can focus on maintenance and stay consistent. Skipping it usually means the first several regular cleans are spent catching up rather than keeping the home clean.

### How long does a deep clean take compared to a regular clean?

A deep clean takes considerably longer than a regular clean of the same home, because it covers areas a regular clean skips, such as inside appliances, behind furniture, baseboards and window tracks. The exact time depends on size, condition, layout and pets. A walkthrough is the most reliable way to know how long your home will take.

### How often should you deep clean a house?

Most homes benefit from a deep clean once or twice a year on top of a regular cleaning schedule. Spring and the weeks before the holidays are common times. If the home is not on a regular schedule, a deep clean every few months helps stop buildup from getting ahead of you. Homes with pets, allergies or heavy traffic may need it more often.

### Does a regular cleaning include inside the fridge and oven?

Usually not. Appliance interiors, such as inside the oven, microwave and refrigerator, are normally part of a deep clean or requested as an add-on to a regular visit. Regular cleans cover appliance exteriors, counters, the sink and the stovetop. If you want interiors handled on a rotating basis, say so at the walkthrough and it can be built into your schedule.

### Can I get a deep clean of just the kitchen and bathrooms?

Yes. A partial deep clean focused on the kitchen and bathrooms is a common request, because those rooms collect grease, soap scum and hard-water buildup fastest. The rest of the home can get a regular clean in the same visit. Tell us your priorities during the walkthrough and the scope and estimate will be built around them.
