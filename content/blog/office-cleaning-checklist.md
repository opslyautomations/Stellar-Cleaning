A good office cleaning checklist splits tasks by frequency: trash, restrooms, break rooms and high-touch points every day; full vacuuming, mopping and dusting weekly; detail work monthly; and deep tasks like high dusting and floor care quarterly. Use the lists below as a starting scope of work, then adjust them to your headcount and traffic.

## How to use this office cleaning checklist

Treat this as a template, not a contract. A six-person professional suite in downtown Albany will not need everything on the daily list, and a busy Salem office with a front counter may need parts of it twice a day. Before you hand a checklist to staff or a cleaning provider, mark each task with:

- **Who does it** — staff, an in-house custodian or an outside crew.
- **How often** — every visit, weekly, monthly or quarterly.
- **Any exclusions** — rooms that are off limits, desks that are not to be touched, equipment that should not be wiped.

That last point matters. The most common complaint about office cleaning is not missed dust; it is a crew touching something it should not have, or skipping something nobody told it about. Writing it down solves both. If you are still deciding on frequency, start with our guide on [how often an office should be cleaned](/blog/how-often-should-an-office-be-cleaned).

## Daily office cleaning checklist

These tasks keep an occupied office sanitary and presentable. In a staffed office they should happen every working day or on every cleaning visit.

### Trash and recycling

- Empty all trash and recycling bins and replace liners.
- Take waste to the building's dumpster or collection area.
- Sort recycling according to your hauler's rules.

### Restrooms

- Clean and disinfect toilets, urinals, sinks and faucets.
- Clean mirrors and wipe partitions where marked.
- Refill toilet paper, paper towels and hand soap.
- Empty sanitary bins and trash.
- Sweep and mop floors.

### Break room and kitchen

- Wipe and sanitize counters, tables and sinks.
- Wipe appliance exteriors and handles: refrigerator, microwave, coffee maker.
- Load or run the dishwasher if that is part of the agreed scope.
- Empty kitchen trash and compost.

### High-touch points

The CDC's [facility cleaning guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/when-and-how-to-clean-and-disinfect-a-facility.html) names door handles, stair rails, elevator buttons, touchpads, restroom fixtures and desks as high-touch surfaces and recommends cleaning them regularly. In an office, your daily high-touch list usually includes:

- Door handles, push plates and light switches.
- Elevator buttons and stair rails.
- Shared phones, conference room remotes and printer touchscreens.
- Reception counters and visitor sign-in areas.

### Floors

- Vacuum entrances, lobbies and main walkways.
- Spot-mop spills and wet entry floors.

Federal sanitation rules, which [Oregon OSHA adopts by reference](https://osha.oregon.gov/rules/final/Pages/division-2.aspx), say workroom floors should be kept dry so far as practicable and that waste must be removed as often as necessary to keep the workplace sanitary ([29 CFR 1910.141](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.141)). That is a good reason to keep entry mopping on the daily list during the rainy months.

## Weekly cleaning tasks

Weekly tasks cover the rest of the office: the rooms and surfaces that do not see constant traffic but still collect dust and dirt.

1. Vacuum all carpeted areas, including private offices and conference rooms.
2. Mop all hard floors throughout.
3. Dust reachable surfaces: shelves, filing cabinets, window sills, picture frames.
4. Wipe cleared desk surfaces, keyboards and monitors only if your office approves it.
5. Clean interior glass, glass doors and partition windows.
6. Wipe down chairs and conference tables.
7. Clean the outside of trash and recycling bins.
8. Clear the break room refrigerator of old food on a set day, with staff warned in advance.

## Monthly detail tasks

Monthly tasks stop the slow build-up that makes an office feel tired even when the daily list is done.

1. Dust and wipe baseboards.
2. Clean air vents and return grilles you can reach safely.
3. Clean inside the microwave and wipe the inside of the refrigerator.
4. Detail restroom grout, partitions and fixtures.
5. Vacuum upholstered chairs and soft seating.
6. Wipe doors, door frames and switch plates top to bottom.
7. Spot-clean walls and scuffs in hallways.

## Quarterly deep-cleaning tasks

Quarterly work is the deep-clean tier. Some offices fold these tasks into a monthly rotation instead of doing them all at once.

1. High dusting: light fixtures, tops of cabinets, ceiling corners and ledges.
2. Clean blinds and window tracks.
3. Machine-scrub or buff hard floors where appropriate for the surface.
4. Deep clean break room appliances and cabinets inside and out.
5. Review carpets. Routine vacuuming does not remove deep soil, and hot-water extraction is specialist work, so book a carpet specialist when traffic lanes start to show.
6. Review resilient floors. Stripping and refinishing is usually scoped as a separate project.

## Disinfecting the right way

Cleaning removes dirt; disinfecting kills germs, and doing one does not replace the other. The CDC recommends cleaning first, then disinfecting where needed, and leaving the disinfectant on the surface for the full contact time listed on the label. A disinfectant only works as advertised when it is used on the surfaces and for the time its label says.

For your checklist, that means:

- Use a product with an EPA registration number. The EPA keeps a page of [selected registered disinfectants](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-registration/selected-epa-registered-disinfectants) you can check against.
- Clean visibly dirty surfaces before disinfecting.
- Keep the surface wet for the label's contact time, then wipe or air-dry as directed.
- Do not mix products.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should be on a daily office cleaning checklist?

A daily office cleaning checklist should cover trash and recycling, restroom cleaning and restocking, break room counters and appliance handles, high-touch points such as door handles and light switches, and vacuuming or mopping entrances and main walkways. These tasks keep an occupied office sanitary between deeper cleans. Larger or client-facing offices may need some of them, especially restrooms, more than once a day.

### What are the high-touch surfaces in an office?

High-touch surfaces in an office are the ones many people handle every day: door handles and push plates, light switches, elevator buttons, stair rails, shared phones, printer and copier touchscreens, conference room remotes, reception counters, and restroom fixtures. The CDC lists many of these in its facility cleaning guidance. Clean them regularly and disinfect them more often in busy spaces or when illness is going around.

### How often should a break room refrigerator be cleaned?

A break room refrigerator should be cleared of old food weekly and wiped inside at least monthly, with the exterior and handles cleaned on every visit. Announcing a fixed clear-out day gives staff time to take home anything they want to keep. Busy kitchens may need the interior cleaned more often, especially after spills or if odors start to build.

### Should office cleaners clean employee desks?

Office cleaners should clean desk surfaces only when they are cleared and only if your office has agreed to it in the scope of work. Professional crews should never move or handle documents, devices or personal belongings. Many offices adopt a clear-desk convention on cleaning nights so surfaces can be wiped properly without anyone touching papers or equipment.

### What is the difference between cleaning and disinfecting an office?

Cleaning removes dirt, dust and some germs from a surface, while disinfecting uses a registered product to kill germs that remain. Disinfectants work best on surfaces that have already been cleaned, and they need to stay wet for the contact time printed on the label. Most office checklists clean everything and disinfect high-touch points and restrooms.
