After hours office cleaning is the better fit for most offices because crews can vacuum, mop and clean restrooms without interrupting anyone, while daytime cleaning works best as a supplement in busy buildings that need restrooms and entrances touched up during the day. The right choice depends on your traffic, your security rules and how much access you are comfortable giving a cleaning crew.

## What after hours office cleaning looks like

Night or evening cleaning is the traditional model, and for good reason. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics notes that many cleaners work evening hours because office buildings are often cleaned while they are empty ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/building-and-grounds-cleaning/janitors-and-building-cleaners.htm)).

A typical evening visit covers the full routine: trash and recycling, restroom cleaning and restocking, break room surfaces, high-touch points, vacuuming and mopping. Because nobody is working, the crew can move through every room in order, use floor equipment in hallways, and leave mopped floors to dry before morning.

Weekend cleaning is a variation of the same idea. It suits offices that close on weekends and want deeper tasks such as high dusting or detailed floor care done when there is more time.

## What daytime cleaning looks like

Daytime cleaning puts a cleaner in the building while people are working. In larger buildings this role is often called a day porter: someone who keeps restrooms stocked, wipes entrances and glass doors, handles spills as they happen, and resets conference rooms between meetings.

Some buildings move all cleaning to daytime hours. More often, daytime coverage is layered on top of a night crew, with the heavy work still done after close.

## Pros and cons of each

### After hours office cleaning

Pros:

- **No disruption.** No vacuum during a client call, no wet floor in the lobby at 10am, no one emptying bins around your desk.
- **Full access to every room.** Conference rooms, private offices and shared spaces are empty and easier to clean thoroughly.
- **Floor work is practical.** Mopping, machine work and drying time do not compete with foot traffic.
- **Privacy.** Staff do not have to share space with an outside crew during confidential work.

Cons:

- **Problems wait until night.** A spill at 9am or an empty paper towel dispenser at noon stays that way unless staff handle it.
- **Access must be managed.** The crew needs keys or cards and usually alarm codes, so you need clear procedures.
- **Less visibility.** You do not see the work being done, so inspections and a direct contact line matter more.

### Daytime cleaning

Pros:

- **Real-time response.** Spills, restroom supplies and entrance floors get handled as they happen.
- **Visible presence.** Visitors see a well-kept space throughout the day.
- **Simpler access.** The building is already open, so no alarm codes or after-hours keys.

Cons:

- **Disruption.** Noise and movement in a working office, especially open-plan spaces.
- **Limited tasks.** Some work, like full floor care, is not practical with people around.
- **Occupied rooms get skipped.** Private offices and meeting rooms in use cannot be cleaned until they are free.

## Security, keys and alarm codes

The main concern with after hours office cleaning is trust: you are giving people access to your building when nobody else is there. Handle it with written procedures, not assumptions.

Agree on these before the first visit:

1. **Who holds keys or access cards**, how many copies exist, and how they are logged.
2. **Individual alarm codes** for the cleaning crew where your system supports them, so entries and exits are traceable and the code can be changed without affecting staff.
3. **Arrival and departure windows**, so an alarm at 2am is clearly not the cleaners.
4. **Lock-up steps**, such as which doors to check, which lights stay on and what to do if a door will not latch.
5. **Who to call** if the alarm trips or something looks wrong.
6. **What happens to keys and codes** if the contract ends.

## Restricted areas: Salem agencies and professional offices

In Salem, a large share of office space belongs to state agencies, professional services firms and healthcare providers. These offices often have rooms where cleaning crews either should not go or should go only under specific conditions: records rooms, server closets, evidence or case-file storage, executive offices and anywhere with confidential client files.

Write restricted areas into the scope. For each one, choose:

- **Excluded entirely** — the crew does not enter.
- **Serviced under escort** — cleaned only when a staff member is present.
- **Serviced with limits** — for example, floors and trash only, no surfaces touched.

For medical and dental offices, HIPAA questions come up often. The US Department of Health and Human Services says janitorial services that clean a covered entity's offices are generally not business associates, because any exposure to protected health information while doing their work, such as when emptying trash, is incidental ([HHS FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/243/is-a-business-associate-contract-required-for-inadvertent-contact-with-phi/index.html)). That does not remove the need for sensible safeguards, so keep files locked away and screens off on cleaning nights. This is general information, not legal advice; check with your compliance lead or attorney.

Choose **after hours office cleaning** if your office is empty in the evening, you want no disruption, and you can set up clear access procedures. This fits most professional offices, agencies and small to mid-size suites.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it better to clean an office at night or during the day?

It is usually better to clean an office at night, because crews can work through every room, run floor equipment and clean restrooms without disrupting staff or visitors. Daytime cleaning is better as an add-on for busy buildings that need restrooms restocked, spills handled and entrances touched up while people are working. Many larger buildings combine both.

### What is a day porter?

A day porter is a cleaner who works in a building during business hours to handle tasks that cannot wait for the night crew. Typical duties include restocking restrooms, wiping entrances and glass doors, cleaning up spills, emptying overflowing bins and resetting conference rooms between meetings. Day porters usually supplement a night crew rather than replace it.

### Should I give my office cleaners an alarm code?

Yes, after-hours cleaners usually need an alarm code, but they should get their own code rather than sharing a staff code. A separate code lets you see when the crew enters and leaves, and you can change it without affecting employees. Agree on arrival windows, lock-up steps and who to call if the alarm trips before the first visit.

### Can cleaners be kept out of certain rooms?

Yes, you can keep cleaners out of specific rooms by writing them into the scope of work as restricted areas. For each room, decide whether it is excluded entirely, cleaned only under escort, or cleaned with limits such as trash and floors only. This is common in state agency, legal, medical and financial offices with confidential files or secure equipment.

### Do after-hours cleaners need to be background-checked?

After-hours cleaners should be background-checked, because they work in your building when no one else is there. Ask any provider whether every crew member is checked before entering client sites, whether they are insured, and whether you will get a consistent crew.
